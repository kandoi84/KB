# Reliance Industries — 2Q26 Preview

**Company:** Reliance Industries Ltd. (RIL)<br>
**Research type:** Pre-earnings preview<br>
**Stored label:** 2Q26 preview<br>
**Information freeze:** 25 Sep 2026

## Executive view

The Q2 setup looks materially better for O2C than the stock performance implies, while Jio remains the cleanest structural compounding leg and Retail remains the biggest debate around earnings quality.

RIL entered Q2 with record recurring EBITDA of ₹54,067 crore in Q1. Q1 O2C EBITDA was ₹17,010 crore, Digital Services ₹21,255 crore, Retail ₹6,309 crore and upstream ₹4,973 crore. Net debt was ₹1.23tn and quarterly capex ₹38,682 crore.

Since then, the most important change is refining. Asian diesel cracks recently exceeded $87/bbl, a record, reflecting supply disruptions and extremely tight middle-distillate markets. That argues against assuming a normal Q2 sequential decline in O2C profitability.

The less obvious offset is below EBITDA: depreciation and finance costs are structurally higher following Jio asset capitalization. In Q1 depreciation was ₹15,100 crore and finance costs ₹8,337 crore, up 9% and 19% YoY respectively. So an EBITDA beat does not translate one-for-one into EPS upside.

**Core framing:** Q2 is primarily an O2C earnings-upgrade test, not a Jio earnings test. For the stock to respond meaningfully, strong refining needs to coexist with evidence that Retail margin dilution is temporary and that capex/new-energy spending will ultimately create measurable EBITDA.

## O2C — main earnings swing factor

Q1 O2C EBITDA rose 17.2% YoY to ₹17,010 crore despite expensive crude sourcing, planned turnaround activity, LPG diversion and other operating constraints.

The Q2 setup became stronger as Asian diesel cracks moved to record levels and global middle-distillate supply remained tight.

The key debate is whether headline refining strength converts into realised economics after crude procurement differentials, freight, feedstock scarcity, LPG obligations and product-placement costs.

### Key O2C KPIs

| KPI | Bullish evidence | Warning sign |
|---|---|---|
| Middle-distillate cracks | High cracks flow through realised margins | Crude/feedstock premiums absorb upside |
| Refinery utilization | Sustained near-full utilization | Supply disruptions restrict crude sourcing |
| Product placement | Exports redirected to highest-value markets | Logistics/freight eat arbitrage |
| Petchem deltas | PE/PP/polyester spreads improve | Chinese capacity keeps margins depressed |
| Ethane economics | Cheap ethane continues to advantage RIL | Gas/feedstock costs rise |
| Production for sale | Rebounds after Q1 turnaround impact | Further operational constraints |

### O2C bar

- Below ₹18k crore EBITDA: disappointing given refining conditions.
- ₹19–20.5k crore: confirms extraordinary cracks are being monetized.
- Above ₹21k crore: likely requires material FY27 O2C estimate upgrades unless management signals rapid normalization.

## Jio — monetization over subscriber acquisition

Q1 JPL revenue from operations reached ₹39,173 crore, EBITDA ₹20,865 crore and EBITDA margin 53.3%. Subscribers reached 533m, including 285m 5G users; fixed broadband connections were 28.6m, of which 14m were AirFiber. ARPU was ₹215.6.

The question is increasingly whether Jio can convert high data consumption and 5G infrastructure into higher revenue per customer without another blunt tariff reset.

| Jio KPI | Q1 FY27 | Q2 watch |
|---|---:|---|
| Subscribers | 533m | Continued additions desirable; quality matters more |
| ARPU | ₹215.6 | ₹217–220+ constructive |
| EBITDA margin | 53.3% | Maintaining >53% important |
| AirFiber | 14m | Strong additions remain a growth engine |
| Digital-services growth | ~20% YoY | Should remain faster than connectivity |
| Data traffic | +26.9% YoY | Monetization matters more than raw traffic |

## Retail — core quality-of-earnings debate

Q1 adjusted Retail revenue grew 11.6% YoY, yet EBITDA declined 1.1% to ₹6,309 crore and EBITDA margin was 7.9%. Management described lower profitability as a conscious investment phase in digital commerce and hyperlocal fulfillment.

The key debate is whether Retail is temporarily sacrificing margin to build a structurally stronger omnichannel platform, or whether quick commerce permanently lowers the economics of the business.

A constructive Q2 would be:
- Revenue +12–15%
- EBITDA +5–10%
- Margin stable or beginning to recover

A weak Q2 would be:
- Revenue +15%
- EBITDA flat/down again

## RCPL / FMCG optionality

Q1 FMCG revenue was about ₹8,600 crore, with more than 5,000 distributors, reach exceeding 3m retail outlets, and over 80% of sales through external channels rather than captive Reliance Retail distribution.

For Q2, the most useful indicators are distribution reach, external-channel mix, beverage share and EBITDA disclosure rather than headline revenue growth alone.

## Upstream

Q1 Oil & Gas EBITDA was ₹4,973 crore, essentially flat YoY.

A ₹4.7–5.1k crore EBITDA outcome is a reasonable base range. The more important question is whether KG-D6 volumes have bottomed.

## Consolidated margin debate

Consolidated EBITDA margin is currently a poor headline KPI because oil-price-driven O2C revenue can distort the denominator.

Segment margins matter more:
- O2C: realised refining margin vs headline cracks
- Jio: EBITDA margin after full 5G capitalization
- Retail: digital-commerce investment vs operating leverage
- FMCG: brand-building investment vs gross-margin scaling
- New Energy: currently capex/cash consumption rather than margin

## EPS-quality issue — depreciation and interest

Q1 consolidated depreciation rose to ₹15,100 crore and finance costs to ₹8,337 crore, partly because Jio's 5G assets are now capitalized and associated interest is expensed rather than capitalized.

Therefore, EBITDA can beat while PAT looks merely in-line. The quarter should be judged more heavily on EBITDA and segment KPIs than on EPS alone.

## New Energy

The key shift required is from capacity announcements to economics:

capacity → utilization → external sales → EBITDA → ROIC

Until then, New Energy remains more relevant to capex and cash consumption than quarterly earnings.

## Stock-moving drivers

1. O2C EBITDA and management's Q3 refining outlook
2. Jio IPO valuation/process commentary and underlying ARPU/margin
3. Evidence that Retail EBITDA growth is catching up with revenue growth
4. New Energy commissioning plus quantified commercial economics
5. Capex/net debt trajectory
6. Below-EBITDA drag from depreciation and interest

## Management questions

1. Your benchmark middle-distillate cracks have strengthened sharply during Q2. How much of that incremental margin is being captured after crude differentials, freight and sourcing costs?
2. At current product cracks, what prevents O2C EBITDA from materially exceeding Q1's ₹17,010 crore?
3. What proportion of Q1's Retail EBITDA pressure represented temporary launch/infrastructure costs versus structurally lower unit economics from hyperlocal delivery?
4. When should digital-commerce incremental EBITDA turn positive?
5. Can Retail double EBITDA by FY29 without recovering EBITDA margin, or does the plan implicitly require margin normalization?
6. Jio digital-services revenue grew ~20%, faster than connectivity. When can investors expect disclosure sufficient to value platform services separately?
7. How much ARPU growth can Jio generate organically from mix, homes and digital services without another wireless tariff increase?
8. With 5G capex now substantially capitalized, where should Jio's depreciation and finance costs normalize?
9. For New Energy, what is the first project that will produce material third-party revenue, and when should it generate positive EBITDA?
10. What is FY27 consolidated capex likely to be, and at what point should New Energy capex peak?
11. Could net debt rise meaningfully even with record operating EBITDA as New Energy/data-center investment accelerates?
12. For RCPL, what proportion of sales now comes through channels outside Reliance Retail, and how does profitability differ between captive and external distribution?

## PM takeaway

The potentially mispriced variable into Q2 is how much of the extraordinary refining environment RIL can actually monetize.

Q1 already demonstrated ₹17,010 crore of O2C EBITDA despite significant operational and sourcing headwinds. That makes ₹19–20k+ crore O2C EBITDA the number to watch most closely.

A strong O2C print alone is not enough to establish better long-term earnings quality. The more important combination is:

**O2C estimates rising + Jio maintaining >53% margins + Retail returning to EBITDA growth + clearer New Energy monetization + capex/net debt remaining controlled.**

That combination would change the earnings path more meaningfully than a simple quarterly EPS beat.
